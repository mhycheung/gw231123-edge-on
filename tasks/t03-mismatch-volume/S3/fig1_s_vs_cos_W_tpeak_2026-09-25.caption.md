Density of distinguishable NRSur7dq4 waveforms per unit prior probability, $s$, against the inclination at the waveform peak, for prior draws in the GW231123 mass window (set W). The plot answers two questions: do orientations that are edge-on at the peak have more distinguishable waveforms per unit prior probability than other orientations, and how does that depend on in-plane spin (precession)?

$s=\sqrt{\det g^\theta}/\pi_\theta(\theta)$, where $g^\theta$ is the mismatch metric of the unit-normalised two-detector (H1, L1) signal over $\theta=(M,q,\vec\chi_1,\vec\chi_2,\iota_Q(t_{\rm peak}))$ with $\phi_{\rm ref}$, $\psi$ and $t_c$ profiled out, and $\pi_\theta$ is the prior density of the parameter estimation (PE) on $\theta$. Both factors are densities on $\theta$, so $s$ does not depend on the coordinates chosen for $\theta$. The mean of $s$ over prior draws in a region is the metric volume of the region divided by its prior probability. $\iota_Q(t_{\rm peak})$ is the angle between the line of sight and the axis of the surrogate's coprecessing frame at $t_{\rm peak}$, the peak of the total mode amplitude (the surrogate's $t=0$; the same quantity is written $\iota_Q(0)$ elsewhere in this task). Horizontal axis: $|\cos\iota_Q(t_{\rm peak})|$ (0 is edge-on, 1 is face-on or face-off); the prior is uniform in it. Vertical axis: $\log_{10}s$, where $s$ has the units of $\theta$ that do not cancel and only ratios of $s$ are meaningful. Points are the centres of six equal-width bins. Solid lines: $\log_{10}$ of the mean of $s$ in the bin. Dashed lines: $\log_{10}$ of the median. Colours: $\chi_p$ at $f_{\rm ref}=10$ Hz in $[0,0.4)$ (blue), $[0.4,0.7)$ (orange), $[0.7,1]$ (green). Shaded bands: bootstrap 68 % intervals (1000 resamples). Dotted vertical line: $|\cos\iota_Q(t_{\rm peak})|=\sin20^\circ$; to its left is the edge-on region $|\iota_Q(t_{\rm peak})-90^\circ|<20^\circ$ used for $R_{\rm edge}$.

Data: 8000 draws from the PE prior of the `C00:NRSur7dq4` label of the GW231123 release, restricted to detector-frame $M\in[287.0,333.2]\,M_\odot$ and $q\in[0.685,0.974]$ (posterior 5–95 %). Sky position fixed at the maximum-likelihood sample; PE PSDs; 20–448 Hz. All 8000 points evaluated with no failures. Derivatives are fourth-order finite differences of the NRSur7dq4 waveform. The same data and bins as `fig1_s_vs_cos_W_2026-09-25.png`; only the axis label differs.

What to see: (1) $s$ falls from edge-on to face-on in every $\chi_p$ bin; the medians fall by 1.8 to 2.4 decades across the axis. (2) In the median, $s$ rises with precession at every inclination: the highest $\chi_p$ bin lies 2.2 to 2.8 decades above the lowest in all six bins (`chi_p_trend_2026-09-25.json`). About 0.8–1.2 decades of this come from $\sqrt{\det g^\theta}$ and about 1.3–1.6 from $1/\pi_\theta$ in the Cartesian spin coordinates used. (3) The rise is roughly the same at all inclinations, so the edge-on to non-edge-on ratio of medians does not rise with $\chi_p$: $R_{\rm edge}=20.7$, 16.5 and 12.2 from low to high $\chi_p$. (4) The means lie 1.5 to 5 decades above the medians and jump from bin to bin: in each region fewer than 1 % of the points hold most of the sum (the 10 largest hold 91 % of the edge-on sum). The mean curves are not converged, and their bands understate the uncertainty.
