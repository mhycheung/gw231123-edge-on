The amplitude of the gravitational-wave strain seen along the line of sight for the maximum-likelihood GW231123 parameters, as predicted by the two NRSur7dq4 surrogate versions; it answers when the observed strain peaks, and whether that peak time $t_*$ is well defined.

The horizontal axis is time $t$ in units of the detector-frame total mass $M = 299.67\,M_\odot$ ($t/M$), from $-300M$ to $+80M$. The vertical axis is $(r/M)\,|h_+ - i h_\times|$ along the line of sight $\hat N$, the dimensionless strain amplitude scaled by distance $r$ over $M$. The blue curve is NRSur7dq4v2 and the orange curve is NRSur7dq4 (v1). The blue and orange dashed vertical lines mark each model's $t_*$, the time of the largest $|h(t, \hat N)|$, found on a $0.1M$ grid and refined by a cubic spline to $0.01M$. The black dotted vertical line marks $t = 0$, the surrogate's time origin, which the surrogate places at the peak of the total amplitude summed over all modes (the project's own grid maximum of the total amplitude is at $-0.8M$ for v2 and $-0.9M$ for v1).

The data are one parameter set: the maximum-likelihood sample (index 6096 of 18185) of the `C00:NRSur7dq4` posterior in Zenodo record 17437902, with detector-frame masses $156.91$ and $142.77\,M_\odot$, spin magnitudes $0.96$ and $0.99$ with both spins nearly in the orbital plane, and inclination $\iota = 64.3^\circ$ at the reference frequency $f_{\rm ref} = 10$ Hz. The strain is the sum $h(t, \hat n) = \sum_{\ell m} h_{\ell m}(t)\, {}_{-2}Y_{\ell m}(\hat n)$ over all modes each surrogate returns (32 for v2, 21 for v1), evaluated in the LAL source frame at $f_{\rm ref}$ with $\hat N$ at polar angle $\iota$ and azimuth $\pi/2 - \phi_{\rm ref}$; the project checked this convention against LALSimulation's NRSur7dq4 (relative $L_2$ difference $6.4\times10^{-5}$). Both spin magnitudes are outside the surrogate training range ($\chi \le 0.8$), so the waveform is an extrapolation, the same one the parameter estimation used.

What to see: near merger $|h(t,\hat N)|$ has three peaks of nearly equal height, at about $t = -27M$, $-13M$ and $-3M$. For v2 their heights relative to the maximum are $1.000$, $0.927$ and $0.991$; for v1 they are $0.982$, $0.912$ and $1.000$. The two models therefore choose different peaks: $t_* = -27.03M$ for v2 and $t_* = -2.69M$ for v1. Because the choice depends on differences of 1 to 2 %, $t_*$ is not a stable reference time for this signal; the inclination at the three peaks is $\iota_Q \approx 82^\circ$, $87$ to $88^\circ$ and $89$ to $90^\circ$ in both models.
