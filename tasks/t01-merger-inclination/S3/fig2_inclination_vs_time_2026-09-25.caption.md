The orbital inclination of the maximum-likelihood GW231123 binary as a function of time, by two definitions and for two surrogate versions; it answers how the inclination changes between the reference frequency, where the parameter estimation defines it, and merger.

Both panels show inclination in degrees against time $t/M$, where $M = 299.67\,M_\odot$ is the detector-frame total mass and $t = 0$ is the surrogate's time origin (the peak of the total amplitude summed over modes). The left panel covers $-1100M$ to $+60M$; the right panel shows $-210M$ to $+55M$ on an expanded scale. Solid lines are $\iota_Q(t)$, the angle between the line of sight $\hat N$ and the axis $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t)$ of the surrogate's coprecessing frame (the frame that follows the precession of the orbital plane), computed from the surrogate's quaternions on the full $0.1M$ time grid. Dashed lines are $\iota_E(t)$, the angle between $\hat N$ and $\hat e(t)$, the direction in which the strain amplitude $|h(t, \hat n)|$ over the sphere of directions $\hat n$ is largest; $\hat e$ is found by a full-sphere search at $t_*$, chosen as the maximum at which the phase of $h$ decreases in time (the $+\hat L$ side in the LAL convention), and then followed from $t_*$ backward and forward at $1M$ steps from $t_* - 1000M$ to $t_* + 50M$. Blue is NRSur7dq4v2 and orange is NRSur7dq4 (v1). The dash-dot vertical lines mark each model's $t_*$, the peak time of $|h(t,\hat N)|$ ($-27.03M$ for v2, $-2.69M$ for v1). The dotted vertical lines mark the time $t_{\rm ref}$ at which the orbit reaches $f_{\rm ref}$, as recorded in the result files ($-195.6M$ for v2, $-198.8M$ for v1); these values come from an orbital-frequency estimate that a later task replaced by a different definition, which changes $t_{\rm ref}$ but none of the angles. The horizontal black dotted line is the parameter-estimation value $\iota = 64.3^\circ$ at $f_{\rm ref} = 10$ Hz.

The data are the maximum-likelihood sample (index 6096) of the `C00:NRSur7dq4` posterior of GW231123 (Zenodo record 17437902), evaluated with each surrogate at $f_{\rm ref} = 10$ Hz from the start of the surrogate. Both spin magnitudes ($0.96$, $0.99$) are outside the surrogate training range ($\chi \le 0.8$). $\iota_E$ is not the same quantity as $\iota_Q$: the modes with odd $m$ tilt the direction of maximum emission away from the orbital axis by several degrees (a project control with aligned spins and mass ratio $1.5$ gave $5.6^\circ$), and this offset turns with the orbital phase; this offset is a possible cause, not tested, of the oscillations of the dashed curves about the solid ones. The project plan notes, citing Ravishankar et al. (2026), that NRSur7dq4v2 freezes the coprecessing frame between $t = 0$ and $t = 20M$; the blue solid line is flat after about $t = +20M$, consistent with this, so $\iota_Q$ of v2 after the peak is not a measurement of the orbital axis. The project does not interpret the curves after merger.

What to see: $\iota_Q$ equals the input $\iota$ at $t_{\rm ref}$ ($64.33^\circ$ for v2 against $64.33^\circ$ input), which is close to the minimum of $\iota_Q$ over the last $1000M$ ($64.27^\circ$); earlier, at $t = -1000M$, $\iota_Q = 85.2^\circ$. After $t_{\rm ref}$ both $\iota_Q$ and $\iota_E$ rise toward edge-on ($90^\circ$) and track each other closely in the two models. $\iota_Q$ reaches its maximum over $t < 20M$ of $89.1^\circ$ (v2) and $90.0^\circ$ (v1) near $t \approx -6M$, and equals $88.7^\circ$ (v2) and $89.5^\circ$ (v1) at $t = 0$. At $t_*$ the values are $\iota_Q = 81.8^\circ$, $\iota_E = 80.4^\circ$ (v2) and $\iota_Q = 89.8^\circ$, $\iota_E = 88.6^\circ$ (v1); the difference between the models comes from their different $t_*$, not from different angles at a given time.
