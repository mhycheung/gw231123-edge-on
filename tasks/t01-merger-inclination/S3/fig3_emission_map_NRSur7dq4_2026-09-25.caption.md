A sky map of the strain amplitude radiated in every direction at the peak time $t_*$ of the observed strain, for the maximum-likelihood GW231123 binary with the older NRSur7dq4 (v1) surrogate; it answers where the line of sight lies relative to the orbital axis and to the direction of strongest emission at that time, and is the v1 counterpart of the NRSur7dq4v2 map.

The map is a Mollweide projection of the direction $\hat n$ in the LAL source frame at the reference frequency $f_{\rm ref} = 10$ Hz ($\hat z$ along the orbital angular momentum at $f_{\rm ref}$, $\hat x$ from the lighter to the heavier black hole). The horizontal coordinate is the azimuth $\phi$ of $\hat n$ and the vertical coordinate is $90^\circ - \theta$, with $\theta$ the polar angle from $\hat z$; both are labelled in degrees. Colour (viridis, scale on the bar below; note the scale differs from the v2 map) is $(r/M)\,|h(t_*, \hat n)|$, the strain amplitude $|h_+ - i h_\times|$ scaled by distance $r$ over the detector-frame total mass $M = 299.67\,M_\odot$, computed from all 21 surrogate modes at $t_* = -2.69M$ on a $1^\circ$ grid. The markers are: red star, the line of sight $\hat N$; white circle, the coprecessing-frame axis $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$; orange cross, $\hat e$, the direction of maximum $|h(t_*,\hat n)|$ on the side where the phase of $h$ decreases in time; magenta triangle, $\hat z(f_{\rm ref})$, the orbital axis at $f_{\rm ref}$ (the north pole of the map). The second, opposite maximum of $|h|$ is not marked.

The data are the maximum-likelihood sample (index 6096) of the `C00:NRSur7dq4` posterior of GW231123 (Zenodo record 17437902), with $\iota = 64.3^\circ$ at $f_{\rm ref}$, so $\hat N$ sits at polar angle $64.3^\circ$ from the map's pole. $t_*$ is the time of largest $|h(t,\hat N)|$ and $t = 0$ is the surrogate's time origin (the peak of the total amplitude). For this sample three peaks of $|h(t,\hat N)|$ lie within 9 % of each other, and v1 picks the latest one, $t_* = -2.69M$, while v2 picks $t_* = -27.03M$; the two maps are therefore at different times. Both spin magnitudes ($0.96$, $0.99$) are outside the surrogate training range ($\chi \le 0.8$). $\hat e$ is not expected to coincide with the orbital axis: the modes with odd $m$ tilt the beam by several degrees.

What to see: between $f_{\rm ref}$ and $t_*$ the orbital axis has precessed from the pole to $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$. The line of sight is $\iota_Q = 89.8^\circ$ from $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$ and $\iota_E = 88.6^\circ$ from $\hat e$, compared with $64.3^\circ$ from $\hat z(f_{\rm ref})$, so at this $t_*$ the view is within $1.4^\circ$ of edge-on by either definition. $\hat e$ is $3.6^\circ$ from $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$. The opposite maximum, the bright region in the southern part of the map, has $0.95$ of the amplitude at $\hat e$ and lies $7.8^\circ$ from $-\hat e$.
