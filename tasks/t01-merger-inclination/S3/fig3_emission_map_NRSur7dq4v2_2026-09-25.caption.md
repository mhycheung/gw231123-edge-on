A sky map of the strain amplitude radiated in every direction at the peak time $t_*$ of the observed strain, for the maximum-likelihood GW231123 binary with the NRSur7dq4v2 surrogate; it answers where the line of sight lies relative to the orbital axis and to the direction of strongest emission at that time.

The map is a Mollweide projection of the direction $\hat n$ in the LAL source frame at the reference frequency $f_{\rm ref} = 10$ Hz ($\hat z$ along the orbital angular momentum at $f_{\rm ref}$, $\hat x$ from the lighter to the heavier black hole). The horizontal coordinate is the azimuth $\phi$ of $\hat n$ and the vertical coordinate is $90^\circ - \theta$, with $\theta$ the polar angle from $\hat z$; both are labelled in degrees. Colour (viridis, scale on the bar below) is $(r/M)\,|h(t_*, \hat n)|$, the strain amplitude $|h_+ - i h_\times|$ scaled by distance $r$ over the detector-frame total mass $M = 299.67\,M_\odot$, computed from all 32 surrogate modes at $t_* = -27.03M$ on a $1^\circ$ grid. The markers are: red star, the line of sight $\hat N$; white circle, the coprecessing-frame axis $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$; orange cross, $\hat e$, the direction of maximum $|h(t_*,\hat n)|$ on the side where the phase of $h$ decreases in time; magenta triangle, $\hat z(f_{\rm ref})$, the orbital axis at $f_{\rm ref}$ (the north pole of the map). The second, opposite maximum of $|h|$ is not marked.

The data are the maximum-likelihood sample (index 6096) of the `C00:NRSur7dq4` posterior of GW231123 (Zenodo record 17437902), with $\iota = 64.3^\circ$ at $f_{\rm ref}$, so $\hat N$ sits at polar angle $64.3^\circ$ from the map's pole. $t_*$ is the time of largest $|h(t,\hat N)|$ and $t = 0$ is the surrogate's time origin (the peak of the total amplitude); for this sample three peaks of $|h(t,\hat N)|$ lie within 8 % of each other, so $t_*$ is sensitive to small model differences (the v1 map uses $t_* = -2.69M$). Both spin magnitudes ($0.96$, $0.99$) are outside the surrogate training range ($\chi \le 0.8$). $\hat e$ is not expected to coincide with the orbital axis: the modes with odd $m$ tilt the beam by several degrees.

What to see: between $f_{\rm ref}$ and $t_*$ the orbital axis has precessed from the pole to $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$, and the emission is brightest around that axis. The line of sight is $\iota_Q = 81.8^\circ$ from $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$ and $\iota_E = 80.4^\circ$ from $\hat e$, compared with $64.3^\circ$ from $\hat z(f_{\rm ref})$; $\hat e$ is $2.9^\circ$ from $\hat z_{\rm copr}(t_*)$. The opposite maximum, in the southern part of the map, has $0.80$ of the amplitude at $\hat e$ and lies $3.5^\circ$ from $-\hat e$. $\hat N$ lies in the region of lower amplitude between the two lobes.
